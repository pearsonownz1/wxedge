1. Replace the stub data with real Kalshi tickers. Every market is named KMDW-STUB-4, KNYC-STUB-3, etc. — those are placeholders, not real Kalshi contracts. Until the app is pulling live tickers (the actual high-temp series on Kalshi's API) with real order books, every number downstream is decorative. This is job one.

2. Fix the implied-probability parsing — there's a real bug here. Look at your Markets board: the brackets for a single station are mutually exclusive, yet the implied percentages read 69%, 66%, 65%, 61%, 60%... Those can't all be true; a coherent book across brackets should sum to roughly 100% (a bit over, due to spreads). Yours sums to ~370%. Classic causes: reading the NO bid as the YES price, using 1 - price on the wrong side, or displaying the same mid for every bracket. Same disease on the Dashboard (Mkt 49/52/55/58/61%...). Related cosmetic bugs while you're in there: timestamps render as "23:51 LST LST" and one banner says "06:20:28 UTC LST" — pick a timezone per label and print it once.

3. Kill the stale feed for real. Both Markets and Dashboard show the stale banner. Whatever the snapshot job is (polling loop died, Replit instance slept, rate limit), this needs to be boring and reliable before anything else matters — a scheduled job with retry and an alert, not a process that dies overnight. Replit's free-tier sleeping behavior is a common culprit; you may want the collector running somewhere persistent even if the UI stays on Replit.

4. Sanity-check the model's confidence. The model prints 99-100% in one bracket and 0% everywhere else (KLAX ≤78° at 100%, KNYC ≤86° at 99%). Meanwhile your own NBM spread panel shows P10-P90 of roughly 79.6-84.4 — a ~5°F band. A single RMSE per station applied at all times of day will do this: by late evening the high is essentially locked and 99% is fine, but if that's the shape at noon, your sigma is too tight. The fix is making error horizon-aware: calibration pairs should be (forecast at lead time H → CLI actual), so the model is wide in the morning and collapses toward certainty as the running high locks in. Your intraday chart already tracks the running high — condition on it: P(final high = x | running high, hour, forecast).

5. Then start the only thing that produces evidence: the snapshot log. Once real data flows, persist every snapshot — timestamp, station, bracket, model %, implied %, best executable YES/NO ask, running high — to a database, every few minutes, for 60+ days. No trading, no paper P&L yet even. That log is what lets you draw the reliability curve (when the model says 70%, does it happen 70%?) and measure whether market prices are actually miscalibrated after fees. Everything you've built is the display layer for that dataset; the dataset doesn't exist yet.

6. Small upgrade for later: your calibration table shows 79-93 pairs per station from one summer window. That's enough to estimate a mean bias, not enough to trust seasonal splits or tail behavior — IEM/ACIS will happily give you 10 years, so backfill when you get a chance.

The one-line version: the UI is ahead of the data, which is the fun-but-dangerous state — it looks like a trading tool while the numbers underneath are stubs, a parsing bug, and an overconfident sigma. Spend the next stretch entirely below the waterline (real tickers → correct book math → reliable collector → horizon-aware sigma → snapshot log), and don't let anyone — including you — act on the "ACTIONABLE" column until the reliability curve from step 5 says the model has earned it. Which, credit where due, is exactly the gate your friend's original document proposed.
