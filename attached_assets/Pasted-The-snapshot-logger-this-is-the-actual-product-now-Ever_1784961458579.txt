The snapshot logger — this is the actual product now. Every 4-minute refresh should persist to a real table (SQLite is fine to start): timestamp, station, bracket bounds, model %, implied bid/ask, running high, hours-to-settlement. Then a small morning job that fetches the CLI final high and writes the outcome row. That closed loop is what generates the reliability curve, and nothing you've built matters until that dataset has ~60 days in it.
Check that your polling survives deployment reality. A setInterval in index.ts only fires while the process is alive — on Replit, dev servers sleep and autoscale deployments spin down between requests. If the collector dies overnight, you'll get Swiss-cheese data and the stale banner you just fixed comes back. Either use a Reserved VM–style always-on deployment or move the collector to a scheduled job; verify by checking the log has unbroken 4-minute rows across a full night.
Put the fee formula into the edge math before anything is labeled ACTIONABLE. Kalshi's trading fee is roughly proportional to price × (1 − price), which peaks exactly in the 40-60¢ zone where interesting disagreements live. An "edge" column that ignores fees will systematically flag phantom opportunities in the most expensive region. (Verify the current formula from Kalshi's fee schedule rather than trusting my constant.)
Horizon-aware sigma, phase two. Conditioning on the running-high floor was the right first move, but sigma itself should still shrink with lead time — the forecast error at 9am is not the error at 8pm. Once the snapshot logger exists, you can fit this empirically from your own data instead of guessing: that's the elegant part, the logger feeds the model that the logger is evaluating.

The mental shift for this phase: the app stops being the deliverable and becomes the instrument. For the next couple of months the deliverable is a boring, gap-free table of predictions and outcomes — and the discipline is that nobody trades off the ACTIONABLE column until that table proves the model's 70% means 70%. You're genuinely close to the interesting part now, which is ironically the part where nothing visible happens.
